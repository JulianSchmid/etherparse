# 17: Ground-truth simulator for reconnects and late segments, with a trace view

Parent: ../spec.md

**Why:** Council rounds 24 to 32 each found one more benign scenario in the same area, late segments of a replaced connection after a reconnect with a nearby ISN, and each fix was followed by a neighbouring scenario (the TIME-WAIT ACK, its RST, a lost FIN, the RST captured first, new ISNs inside the old spans). The scenario space is a product of several dimensions, and two properties of the test setup kept it dark: the model proptests never generate near-ISN reconnects (ISNs of different scripts lie 2^30 apart, a decision of ticket 10), and the reference model mirrors the code's rules, so it can only catch code-versus-model mismatches, never a wrong rule. The fixes also made C1 long. A simulator with ground truth replaces the reviewers as the source of scenarios, and a trace view lets a human see what the pool did with a failing case.

**What to build:**

1. **A world generator** (`etherparse/src/tcp_reassembly/simulator.rs`, test-only like `reference_model.rs`): endpoints that behave like TCP stacks do, producing the true segment sequence of a *history* of connections on one four-tuple, each segment labelled with the real connection it belongs to and the offsets of its bytes. Per connection: handshake (or none: the capture starts mid-stream), data in both directions with window scaling, close (FIN exchange with the last ACK, simultaneous close, or an RST by either side, or no close), and then a reuse of the four-tuple by the next connection with an ISN chosen like stacks choose it:
   - far away (random), as today;
   - Linux TIME-WAIT reuse: the new client ISN is the old `snd_nxt + 65537` and the server accepts;
   - near and below the old end (RFC 6528 clock behind a fast stream): the server in TIME-WAIT answers the SYN with an ACK of the old FIN, the client replies `<SEQ=SEG.ACK><CTL=RST>` without ACK flag and retransmits the SYN with the same ISN after the TIME-WAIT socket died;
   - reuse after an RST or a crash without TIME-WAIT: ISNs drawn inside the old spans;
   - ISNs near 2^32 so the numbers wrap.
   The world also emits what real stacks emit late: a retransmitted FIN in LAST-ACK, an older pure ACK, bytes in flight when the peer aborted, retransmissions of the handshake.
2. **A capture layer** that turns the true sequence into what a capture shows: loss of any segment (also the SYN-ACK, the FINs and the last ACK), reordering with a bounded skew (merged taps), the capture starting at a random segment (pickup), one direction only, duplicates. Every captured segment keeps its label.
3. **A truth oracle**, independent of the rules in `reference_model.rs`: after feeding the capture, for every real connection whose handshake (or first bytes) was captured, exactly one pool connection carries it, with its captured bytes readable in order, without fake gaps (a gap only where the capture lost bytes), with the end the world produced (`Finished`, `Reset`, `Reconnect` when the next connection started before the close was captured), and no ghost connection exists (every pool connection maps to a real one; ids are consecutive). Cases that are genuinely ambiguous for a passive observer are excluded by the generator, each exclusion with a comment naming the spec's known limit. The generator never produces hostile traffic.
4. **A trace view**: a text timeline of a scenario, one line per segment with its time, direction, flags, sequence and ACK numbers relative to each real connection's ISNs, the real connection it belongs to, and the pool's decision (which connection took it, dropped by S5, absorbed as a late segment, started a reconnect, out-of-window) with each stream's state after it (start, highest, acked, holes, end). The proptest prints the trace of the minimal failing case. An HTML sequence diagram rendered from the same trace is the second step, once the text form is useful.
5. **Coverage**: the generator enumerates the near-ISN dimensions deliberately (the ISN relations above × which close packets were lost × the capture order of the late segments) rather than hoping random draws hit them; the random dimensions stay random.

**Then:** with the simulator green, try removing rules of C1 one by one (mutation testing against the simulator) and keep only the ones a scenario needs. Rewrite C1 from the surviving rules.

**Blocked by:** none (the code of tickets 01 to 12 is implemented; 14 overlaps, the simulator is its reconnect half with a truth oracle)

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [ ] `simulator.rs` generates histories with all ISN relations listed above and the late segments stacks emit, with labels

## Comments

**2026-11-08 — created during the council review (rounds 24 to 32).** The round 32 fixes (expected-position precedence, the span rule gated on an incomplete handshake, the SYN-SENT RST) are the last rule patches before this ticket; the unit tests written during the rounds (TIME-WAIT with and without the lost close, both capture orders, the merged-tap last ACK, older ACKs, in-flight bytes, FIN retransmissions, overlapping spans after a reset, the one-direction FIN) are the seed scenarios the simulator has to reproduce. The owner decided on 2026-11-08 to build the simulator instead of continuing the round-by-round fixes.

**2026-11-08 — simulator results (`simulator.rs`, tests `reconnect_matrix` and `reconnect_histories`):** the 960-scenario matrix and the random histories pass at 3000 cases and on ten fixed seeds, with an eager reader. What the simulator found and what was done:
- Rules the code needed (C1): late segments are absorbed only while the sender's stream has no start in the reconnect (on one path, an endpoint's old segments can't arrive after its first new one), which replaced the "sent nothing" condition and the handshake gate; the SYN-SENT RST rule wins over the expected-position precedence (an absorbed ACK can move the expected position onto the RST); the old spans extend by k windows past their ends for bytes the capture lost (which subsumed the earlier "at the end or one after it" rule and the move of the end by absorbed bytes, both removed); a stream the capture never saw has no first byte but stands at its peer's kept ACK (K1). C4: a SYN in a pickup's A5 range after a FIN of the connection starts a reconnect (a late own SYN never arrives after a FIN).
- Rules removed as redundant: a mutant check against the simulator at 3000 cases decides; the kept-ACK position survived such a check once and was then needed by a later case, so a surviving mutant is evidence, not proof.
- World constraints (realism, not limits): an endpoint's late segments arrive before its first segment of the next connection; the capture's skew keeps each direction's order; a client in SYN-SENT drops an ACK that ACKs its SYN instead of answering with an RST (RFC 9293 3.10.7.3); a reuse never repeats the old ISN exactly; the pool runs without a hole limit (losses of single bytes exceed the default, and the limits are the reference model's domain).
- Known limits the oracle skips, each named in `known_limit` and in C1/C4 of the spec: a lost SYN with the SYN-ACK captured after the client's bytes (the tombstone absorbs them); late segments of a connection the pool never saw (the capture started at its close); the old end coinciding with the new ISN + 1; both new ISNs inside the old spans with a lost handshake half (and lost bytes); a handshake half in the A5 range of a pickup without a FIN before it (the reconnect starts at the other half or not at all).
- Oracle shape: eager reading after every segment (so C4's "read from" condition is live); ends and bytes are sets of acceptable outcomes where a close completes after a replacing SYN; a single byte right before the peer's ACK arriving at a stream without a start is a keep-alive filler (D3) and not expected.
- Reference model: two mismatches of its offset mapping after a resync (the span's first byte can lie before offset 0) were fixed on the way.

**Open (owner):** the simulator's default windows are large (4 MiB fallback), so the k-window tolerances are 8 MiB; a run with small windows and window scaling is a follow-up, as are delayed ACKs (an ACK that lags the bytes it ACKs) in the world generator and the HTML sequence diagram of the trace.

**2026-11-08 — council round 33 (GPT) on the simulator:** three findings, all taken: (1) the C4 FIN tie-breaker misfired on a SYN-ACK a merged capture shows after the client's request and FIN (the SYN lost): a handshake half right at the stream's start (its first byte, or where the peer's kept ACK puts a stream without a start) is the pickup's own also after a FIN; the capture constraint that kept such a SYN-ACK in place is gone, and the tombstone case it protected is a `known_limit` entry instead (lost SYN, bytes before the SYN-ACK, a visible connection before). (2) `SeqNumbers::covers` wrapped the span's length when span plus slack reached a whole cycle of the sequence numbers; the length is kept wide now (unit test with a span of 2^32 - 32768). (3) The oracle compared bytes only; it now compares the gaps too (position among the bytes and length) against the captured truth: the stream's start (SYN, SYN-ACK's ACK number for the client, or first byte), the furthest byte, the FIN and the peer's ACKs (clamped to a captured FIN, which is no byte; an accepted RST's ACK counts), snapshotted at a replacement for the second delivery variant. GPT's mutation (an invented trailing byte after every store) fails the simulator now.

**2026-11-08 — council round 33 (Opus), and what the simulator found after it:** Opus scored 8.6 (correctness 8.5, performance 9.2, robustness 9.0, understandability 8.4). All six findings were taken:
1. C4's FIN rule split benign connections: it also applied to a pickup's stream without a start, where a merged capture shows the late SYN after the other side's FIN. It now applies only to a stream that started from its sender's bytes or FIN (pool tests for both closing sides).
2. The gate "the sender's stream has no start" closed at the SYN-ACK's ACK number (A2) before the client's own late RST arrived (a client that aborted and reconnected at once, its tap lagging). The gate is now "the sender sent nothing the connection took" (a segment it didn't drop).
3. The simulator hid scenarios: one global skew, small; broad exclusions; silent skips. Now each direction has its own skew of up to 8 positions, bounded below the retransmission timeout so each direction keeps its order. The exclusions are narrowed to what the pool's rules decide (`inside_spans`, `overlapping_numbers`, and the C4 check on the first captured half with the last kept ACK). The matrix (8 variants, 1920 scenarios) asserts that no scenario hits a limit but the one listed, and `known_limits_are_rare` bounds the limit hits to 5% of 2000 random histories.
4. The precedence (the new connection's own segments first) was dead under the old gate. It is live under the new one and was narrowed: a sender whose begin the new connection knows is judged by position, within what its stream is known to have sent; otherwise by ACK (an old RST's ACK fell into the new server's sent range).
5. Docs: `process_segment` names the late-segment rule, `own_late_syn_of_pickup` became `after_close`, and C2's limit says what happens once A5 took a reconnect's SYN.
6. Performance: the late-segment check returns at once without a replaced connection, and it computes the k-window slack only while the gate is open.

The simulator then found, at 3000 cases and on fresh seeds:
- A rule, and the simpler one it ended at:
  - A reuse 1 ms after a close (the ISN clock 15625 ahead), whose SYN-ACK the capture lost, with the client's tap lagging: the server's response ACKs the request before the capture shows it, past the old client's end. The k windows of slack past the old spans took it for a late old segment.
  - A first fix clipped the slack at where the other side's stream begins in the new connection. Three later cases broke it: the capture lost the old stream's last bytes, or showed its FIN before the pool had a connection (A6), and the new ISN lay in what the capture didn't show. So the old end the pool knew was too short, and the clip turned late old ACKs into the new connection's segments, one of them into a ghost reconnect. Marking the old end with the RST's sequence number fixed one of the three and needed a further rule for pure ACKs.
  - The rule that stays: no slack past a captured FIN, after which the sender sends nothing (`SeqNumbers::fin_seen`). It is exact, handles the reuse after a close, and needs neither the clip nor the RST mark. The price is a reuse 1 ms after an abort or a crash (no FINs), with the SYN-ACK lost and the client's tap lagging, which joins the "new taken for old" limit; the matrix lists it.
  - Its mirror image: a mid-stream pickup's first byte is only where the capture started, and the late RST of a client ACKed bytes before it, so the RST reset the new connection. The slack now reaches before an old stream's first byte too, unless its SYN was observed. An observed SYN and a seen FIN are a stream's exact start and end; everywhere else the span is widened by k windows.
- The SYN-SENT exception was too wide. A client that crashed answers the server's next segment from CLOSED with an RST without ACK (RFC 9293 3.10.7.1), and the exception absorbed that RST when the capture had lost the SYN-ACK, so the new connection never ended. SYN-SENT now also requires that the sender ACKed nothing yet (the other stream has no kept ACK, K1).
- A pickup's tombstone knows no ISN, so the late SYN-ACK of a pickup that ended with an RST (the server's tap lagging) started a ghost connection. A SYN or SYN-ACK right at a pickup stream's first byte is now the ended pickup's own late handshake, as C4 already says for a live pickup. This changes the rule "any SYN or SYN-ACK creates a new connection on a pickup's tombstone" (C1, an owner decision to confirm). Where the capture lost the first bytes too, the half still starts a new connection ("late pickup handshake"); a wider range would let a tombstone swallow a real reconnect.
- Four oracle bugs:
  - The snapshot at a replacement took the stream starts from the end of the capture, so a late SYN-ACK of the old connection gave the snapshot a start it didn't have yet.
  - `inside_spans` counted segments of the lost half that arrived before the reconnect started, which the connection before takes.
  - The C4 check counted a FIN that arrived before the pool had a connection (A6).
  - The C4 check took a late SYN-ACK for an observed start of the client's pickup, which the eager reader had read already, so A5 couldn't take it.
- Known limits, widened (spec C1):
  - "segments before the handshake": segments captured before either half of the handshake go to the connection before (P3). It replaces "lost SYN", which covered only bytes with the SYN lost. An open connection before also takes such a segment's ACK number, which can make it refuse its own later segments. An RFC 9293 receiver would drop a segment outside its window together with its ACK (3.10.7.4), but the pool applies ACKs first, and changing that touches the S and R rules; it is an owner decision.
  - "new taken for old": also within the slack past an old stream whose FIN the capture didn't show.
- Mutant checks, all killed:
  - slack past a seen FIN: the unit test, the pool test of the reuse after a close and the matrix;
  - no slack before a pickup's first byte: the unit test and the histories;
  - the SYN-SENT exception without the kept ACK: the pool test of the crashed client and the histories;
  - the ACK test also for a known begin: the model and the histories;
  - the exact next position instead of the known-sent range: the model and the matrix;
  - dropped segments counted as sent: the model only, as the simulator produces no dropped old segment before an absorbed one;
  - no first-byte exception in C4: the model and a stream unit test.
- Limit hits in 2000 random histories (4.2% in all, the bound is 5%): segments before the handshake 53, C4 pickup 15, new taken for old 8, old taken for new 4, unseen connection 2, coinciding end 1.

**2026-11-08 — council round 34 (GPT 8.8: correctness 8.5, performance 9.3, robustness 9.2, understandability 8.7):** two findings, both about evidence order, both taken:
1. The pickup first-byte rule of round 33 overrode the exact evidence: a pickup's first captured byte is only where the capture started, so a reconnect's ISN + 1 can lie right there without repeating an ISN, and the old connection's observed SYN, which the new SYN-ACK doesn't ACK, was ignored. The reconnect's SYN-ACK and its response were absorbed. The random histories found it.
2. C4's FIN rule overrode a SYN-ACK that ACKs the client's observed SYN when its own stream was already a pickup: one reordered within its direction behind the server's next bytes and the client's FIN started a ghost reconnect. The simulator kept each direction's order, so it couldn't produce this.

Fixes: one order of evidence for the live connection (`TcpConnection::verdict`) and for tombstones and replaced connections (`SeqNumbers::is_own_handshake`): the sender's observed ISN, then, for a SYN-ACK, the other stream's observed SYN, then the pickup heuristics (first byte, A5 range, FIN). Pool tests for both reproductions; a mutant restoring either old order fails them. The simulator now also holds back a handshake half behind its sender's next segments of the same connection (`Capture::half_delay`, in one history of eight). That needed an oracle fix: a late half of a pickup that the eager reader read changes nothing, its ACK number included. It also gave two named limits: "new taken for old" for a late half too, and "C4 late own half" (no observed SYN, after a FIN). Further runs on fresh seeds found four more gaps in the oracle and its exclusions, none in the pool:
- A FIN starts a stream without a start (A4), so a late SYN-ACK after it is ignored too.
- A late SYN-ACK that the pool ignores gives the initiator's stream no start either (A2). One helper, `observed_start`, now decides this for all detectors.
- The ACK part of "segments before the handshake" now follows the pool's ACK handling. Such an ACK misleads the open connection before in two cases: it becomes the kept ACK of a stream without a start, or it lies within k windows past what the capture showed of the stream, so that stream takes it as an ACK of bytes the capture lost.

Final runs: the matrix, 3000 random histories, and 1500 on each of eight fresh seeds are green, and the model is green at 3000 cases and on four seeds. Limit hits in 2000 random histories: 4.7%.
